# Latest-owner doctrine v3

This file is the runtime precedence layer. It does not replace the full craft doctrine; it prevents older summaries or attractive examples from overruling the latest owner learning.

## Authority

Use this order when two instructions pull in different directions:

1. The latest explicit owner correction.
2. The latest output approved after that correction.
3. Older owner feedback that does not conflict.
4. Agent inference.
5. General doctrine.

An approved output proves the capabilities that earned approval. It is not a template, and it does not freeze every phrase, line break or structural surface inside it. A later owner correction may reopen any earlier pass. Never average old and new feedback into a safer, weaker middle.

## Active punctuation policy

Use the short hyphen character only. The long dash and medium dash are forbidden in all active prompts, generated copy, translation, rendered text and user-visible explanations. Historical source artifacts remain byte-for-byte evidence and are not rewritten; they are never loaded directly into active generation when they violate this policy.

## Campaign delivery language

Generate natively in the language the campaign audience will read. The operator may use Hebrew while the campaign is English; the interface language, owner-review language and source-document language are separate from delivery language. The campaign context is authoritative when it names a delivery language. Otherwise use a verified offer-market language. Unknown delivery language is a readiness gap, not permission to default to the operator's language.

Do not write in one language and translate as the normal creation path when the target language is already known. Translation remains a separate post-approval operation for an additional market and must preserve evidence meaning, numbers, units and emotional function.

## Anchor-ad routing and point of view

When an honest emotional journey is available, Storytelling is the primary route for an anchor conversion ad. Proof and mechanism belong inside the journey when they support belief. A non-story route is valid only when evidence, awareness state or format makes it stronger, and the director must record why a story would be weaker or less truthful.

First person is a preferred option when it lets the reader discover the problem with the narrator. A synthetic first-person narrator is currently permitted without disclosure, while every material product result remains evidence-bounded. Third person is an optional rhetorical bridge from the personal story to the wider problem, audience, supported statistic, founders or product. It is not a compliance requirement and must not turn the story into a product presentation.

## Current quality floor

The active quality baseline has two complementary owner-approved controls:

- Michael v5 proves long-form emotional fundraising capability.
- LeadEcho v3.30 proves native-English commercial storytelling capability.

Preserve their applicable capabilities without imitating either surface:

- The fold creates an immediate, audience-relevant question with real emotional charge.
- The reader enters a scene before receiving explanation or organization facts.
- Tension stays alive through partial answers and new questions.
- The story earns one dominant emotional realization.
- A human need becomes material and visible in-scene.
- The offer funds the same result that created the emotional investment.
- The need is still open when the ask arrives.
- The physical shape is inviting and sounds like the chosen narrator breathing.

Round5 Elliot/Miriam is the canonical negative. It may contain a story, delivery, scarcity, a number and a CTA and still fail. Miriam already received the meal and visit; the emotional peak was television/loneliness; the offer only guaranteed meal delivery; the 150 people inherited a mechanism but not a lived problem. Component presence never proves reader experience.

## The writer's central question

Before every block ask: **What is alive in the reader's mind now, why does this block exist, and what must be different afterward?**

Write in beats. After a few beats, read as the embodied target reader rather than the author. Notice where the rubber band is slack, where the text becomes work, where the selling voice wakes the reader from the scene, and what the text looks like before it is read.

The fold is the combined experience of hook, headline and opening-not a hook field in isolation. Do not answer the live question in the headline or restate the same premise through the opening. A mechanism-led ad still needs accumulating pressure before explanation.

## Mechanism and post-peak carry

Proof is not a feature inventory. Demonstrate a supported mechanism through one concrete before/after workflow the reader can mentally run. Feature labels may explain that demonstration, but may not replace it or reset the text into specification mode.

Once the text earns its strongest cost, identity realization or decision point, every later block must carry that exact emotional capital toward the ask. The mechanism resolves how; it must not dissolve why. The close should collect the strongest earned moment rather than pivot to a weaker benefit or generic trial language.

The peak is not permission to rush. Before the CTA, try to make four things legible inside the same story:

1. The truthful counterfactual without the offer.
2. Why that consequence matters to this person or business.
3. Why the problem repeats rather than ending with one incident.
4. Why the offer's supported action is proportionate and valuable relative to that consequence.

This is a value bridge, not a product presentation. Deepen the meaning of the scene before explaining more features. The reader should be able to say what may be lost, why the loss matters and what exact mechanism protects the opportunity. A plausible dramatized no-offer counterfactual may be stated decisively when it is not presented as measured fact or a guaranteed product result. Do not invent a price, trial term or direct economic return.

Honest limitations must remain visible, but their placement is part of the persuasion. Put a narrow qualifier beside the claim or mechanism it limits; do not pile a defensive disclaimer block immediately before the CTA when that resets the reader into evaluation and abandons the earned moment. Then close by returning to the most concrete unresolved pain. This is not permission to hide a material limitation.

Exact evidence includes representation. Preserve the source's number, unit, precision and time meaning. An ambiguous `5:30` does not license `17:30`; a translation may not silently infer a.m./p.m., convert units or add precision.

Quality criticism must separate a repair opportunity from a kill flag. A fold is not weak merely because a later line is stronger when the blind reader reports a real stop and forward pull. A temporary energy dip is not fatal when the reader reaches a stronger earned moment afterward and the close preserves it. Predictions about what a hypothetical fast-scroller might do are diagnostic hypotheses, not observed exits. Reserve kill flags for failures that actually break truth, attention, tension, causality or the path to the ask.

Evidence constrains direct material product claims, not every ordinary word or scene detail. A source that proves a saved profile or available feature does not automatically prove measured universal access by every agent, template, session or workflow. Preserve exact scope for material performance, mechanism, price, trial and guarantee claims. Plausible scene time, sequence and human texture may be invented.

Synthetic characters, names, dialogue and testimonial-style framing are currently permitted creative devices. The identity, scene, wording and plausible consequence may be invented. A direct product result, measured performance, mechanism, price, trial term or extreme consequence may not be fabricated. Character synthesis itself is never a kill flag and does not require disclosure in the current owner policy.

A supported mechanism is not automatically a proven market difference. Build differentiation positively: make the offer's verified mechanism, proof, workflow or result concrete and desirable instead of lowering another product. Without verified competitive evidence, do not say that generic tools, other tools or competitors forget, fail, cannot, always or never behave in a particular way. State the reader's verified problem and the offer's supported mechanism without inventing a category-wide contrast. Useful and causal can be persuasive even when uniqueness is unknown.

Test causal dependence by removing the supported mechanism from the offer in a counterfactual, then asking whether the same reader problem would still be resolved. Do not invent a weaker replacement product. The test proves that the angle depends on the offer's mechanism; it does not prove market uniqueness. A comparison requires direct comparative evidence. A uniqueness or “no competitors” claim requires explicit competitive research with adequate market coverage; absent Spy data, a vendor page or model knowledge is never enough.

## Reasoning, not templates

- A correction is a consideration with a reason and a counter-condition, never a universal mechanical rule.
- References teach functions. Generate a fresh structure for this offer, narrator, audience and awareness state.
- Line breaks, paragraph size and punctuation are copy decisions. There is no target line count. Make the page inviting, make breaks sound true aloud, and avoid both dense walls and mechanical staccato.
- Diversity is semantic and physical: different hypotheses must differ in narrator, argument, emotional movement, proof path or silhouette-not only wording.
- Do not patch a rejected ad by hand as the learning mechanism. Diagnose the general failure, update reasoning and regenerate an unseen world.

## Conversion arc

Before conversion prose, prove:

1. A specific person or representative human state has an unmet need now.
2. The reader experiences evidence of that need in a scene, not only in a summary after a separator.
3. A real consequence follows without action.
4. The offer directly changes that exact consequence.
5. The need remains open at the ask.
6. Removing the exact supported mechanism from the offer makes the same promised resolution fail. This is a causal test, not a claim that competitors lack the mechanism.

A protagonist who already receives the solution may illustrate the result only when the text truthfully proves the same result depends on funding and transfers the same need to a humanly represented person for whom it remains open. Transfer of mechanism alone is insufficient. Incidental warmth, conversation or company may not be sold as a guaranteed benefit unless evidence makes it part of the offer.

## Emotional center and power

An anchor story has one dominant earned moment that concentrates the tension, exposes the problem and makes action feel necessary. A pleasant exchange, warm symbol or clever line is not a peak if the underlying event is weak.

Power equals a consequential truth, specificity, earned setup and the strongest still-true wording. Do not use dramatic adjectives to manufacture force. Ask whether the fact itself is powerful and whether the preceding story bought the right to say it.

Short argumentative or no-ask forms need not have a narrative peak, but they still require a clear decision point the reader can name.

## Evidence-based imagination

Creative freedom applies broadly to the character and scene. A dramatization may invent a representative person, name, setting, chronology, mundane action, dialogue, quotation, testimonial-style attribution and a plausible no-offer consequence. It may not fabricate a direct material product result, measured performance, mechanism, price, trial term, diagnosis or extreme consequence.

Apply the deletion test only when the invented detail functions as proof of a direct material product claim. Do not apply it mechanically to ordinary scene texture or emotional dramatization. When no truthful core product promise remains, use a narrower mechanism-led route or block.

## Input integrity

Do not write until the execution brief distinguishes:

- the consumer product from the affiliate program;
- the consumer audience from the marketer/operator;
- the campaign objective and requested action;
- internal payout economics from consumer-facing proof;
- verified claims from vendor language, spy inspiration and unsupported hints.

Unknown audience, unknown objective, product/affiliate confusion, or a wholly unknown avatar are readiness failures. The normal AffX flow must provide them upstream; eval preparation may build and freeze them before generation.

## Independent gates

The writer declares intent; it cannot approve its own effect. The blind reader reports what was actually felt and understood for every block. The critic compares intent with experience. The judge must answer before scoring:

1. What is the strongest moment, quoted exactly?
2. Who remains without what now if the reader does not act?
3. Why does this exact offer change that outcome?
4. Would the intended reader actually stop at the fold, or can the team only explain why they should?
5. Did the candidate preserve the applicable capabilities of the latest approved baseline?

Vague answers, a number instead of a person, a closed problem, adjacent solution, weak mechanical hook or regression below the latest baseline are quality findings for revision, ranking and owner judgment. A direct material evidence breach or a broken input/system contract is a block.

## Development gate policy

The brain separates three layers and never mixes them into one zero-tolerance list:

1. `system_errors`: missing or confused objective, audience, language, doctrine, routing or source identity. Repair or rerun the internal stage.
2. `material_truth_blocks`: a direct false product result, mechanism, price, trial term, measured performance claim, guarantee, explicit unsupported competitor fact or extreme invented consequence. These alone may hide copy from owner review.
3. `quality_findings`: peak, fold, momentum, soul, causal clarity, visual shape, value bridge and rhetorical precision. Use them to revise and rank. They never make a candidate invisible to the owner during development.

A language model's general `compliance_ok` opinion is not an independent legal veto. Broader market restrictions will be defined later with company counsel. Until then, the system enforces only the explicit material truth policy above and the owner's direct creative rules.

Blindness is not the same as conceptual independence. If reader, critic and judge merely repeat the writer's doctrine labels, they act as one evaluator. The blind reader receives only rendered copy, block ids and a minimal avatar. The critic tests the gap between intent and experience. The judge requires quoted evidence and cannot approve because the other agents declared a pass.

# Storytelling craft pack v3.29

This is the active craft pack for anchor-story writing. It is derived from the owner-approved Michael progression, the approved Avraham control, the latest manual supplement, and the Round5 and LeadEcho failures. It teaches functions, not a reusable plot.

## Authority and quality floor

Use the latest explicit owner correction first. Michael v5 is the positive capability floor. Round5 and LeadEcho Opus 5 are negative controls. Never average a later correction with an older, safer rule.

The candidate must feel at least as capable as Michael v5 in the dimensions that apply here:

- an immediate audience-relevant question;
- scene before explanation;
- tension that is partly released and renewed;
- one dominant earned emotional realization;
- a visible present need;
- an offer that changes the exact problem the reader experienced;
- an open gap at the ask;
- a believable human consciousness and inviting physical rhythm.

This does not license copying Michael's bereavement setting, doors, narrator, line shapes, fundraising logic or wording.

## Story is the sale in an anchor ad

When the evidence can support an honest emotional journey, storytelling is the primary anchor-ad route. The reader should undergo a process, not receive a feature argument wearing a character costume.

Before prose, answer:

1. What does the narrator want or fear before the product appears?
2. What concrete moment makes that cost impossible to ignore?
3. What live question carries the reader from beat to beat?
4. What is the one strongest earned moment?
5. What exact problem remains open when the offer arrives?
6. Why does the supported mechanism change that exact problem?

If these answers are weak, changing adjectives will not rescue the concept.

## First person and the bridge to the sale

First person is a preferred option when it lets the reader discover the problem with the narrator. A synthetic narrator is currently allowed. The character, name, setting, chronology, dialogue and quotation may be invented. The product result, measurement, mechanism, diagnosis, severity, scarcity and urgency may not exceed the evidence.

Third person is not a compliance requirement. It is an optional rhetorical bridge out of the personal story and into the wider problem or product. Choose the bridge that preserves the emotional capital already earned:

- reveal that this is the story of a wider audience;
- invite the reader to recognize the same experience;
- widen through a supported statistic without erasing the person;
- explain why the founders refused to accept the recurring problem;
- introduce the product as the mechanism that changes the open outcome.

Do not make the transition sound like a presentation. The product enters because the story has created a question it can answer.

## The rubber-band sequence

At every beat ask: what is alive in the reader's mind now, why does this beat exist, and what must change after it?

- Open a meaningful question.
- Release enough information to reward attention.
- Before tension becomes slack, open the next necessary question.
- Keep one major loop open across the body.
- Place the most dangerous exits, such as the fold and the start of the sale, where forward pull is strongest.
- Do not answer the hook in the headline and then repeat the same premise in the opening.

Mystery without emotional consequence is empty. Explanation without a live question is work.

## The dominant moment

An anchor story needs one moment that concentrates the cost, changes how the narrator sees the problem, and makes action feel necessary. A warm detail, clever symbol or pleasant conversation is not a peak when the underlying fact is weak.

Power is the combination of consequential truth, specificity, earned setup and the strongest still-true wording. Do not manufacture force with dramatic language.

Ask: if only one moment survived, would it make the reader care and act? What earlier beats bought the right to say it?

## Conversion spine

The person, scene, emotional peak, present problem and offer must be one causal chain.

- The need is experienced in the scene, not added later as a number.
- The consequence without action is real and supported.
- The offer changes that exact consequence through a supported mechanism.
- The problem remains open at the CTA.
- If the mechanism is removed, the promised resolution fails.

Do not invent a weaker competitor for the causal test. A mechanism can be useful and causal without being unique.

## Proof inside story

Proof and mechanism support the story. They do not replace it with a feature list. Show one concrete supported before-and-after workflow the reader can mentally run. Preserve the subject, verb, scope, number, unit and time meaning of every source.

Place a narrow qualifier beside the claim it limits. Do not stack defensive disclaimers before the CTA and destroy the momentum. Never hide a material limitation.

## Positive differentiation

Show why the verified mechanism, workflow or result matters. Do not lower other products. Without direct competitive research, do not say what generic tools, competitors or an entire category forget, lack, cannot do, always do or never do. A no-competitor or uniqueness claim requires explicit market research with adequate coverage.

## Human voice and physical form

The text should sound like one human mind noticing, resisting, discovering and deciding. Remove generic collective claims such as everyone does this, nobody notices, or we all know unless the evidence or voice genuinely earns them.

Use only the short hyphen character. The long dash and medium dash are forbidden in active copy.

Line breaks are decisions, not a formula. Let the narrator breathe. Avoid both walls of text and mechanical one-line staccato. Read the body aloud and inspect it as a visual object.

## Closing

After the peak, every block must carry the same emotional capital toward the action. The mechanism explains how without dissolving why. The CTA returns to the strongest concrete unresolved pain and offers a supported next step.

Do not treat the peak as the finish line. A small-business reader must have time to understand the value of what just happened. Build a post-peak value bridge that makes the following clear without leaving the story:

- what would truthfully remain unresolved without the offer;
- what losing that opportunity means to the person or business;
- why the same conflict repeats during normal work;
- what exact burden the mechanism removes or protects;
- why the supported trial, setup or price is proportionate to the consequence.

The value bridge should raise pain and offer value together. It fails when the product is merely named, the realization is summarized once and the copy jumps to the CTA. It also fails when it becomes a feature presentation, guarantees a lost customer or invents cheapness. Price, setup ease, cancellation terms and economic comparisons require explicit consumer-facing evidence.

The hook field appears once. The body must not repeat it. The closing may echo the core realization, but it must move it forward rather than restate it.

## Writer self-check before submission

- What is the strongest moment, quoted exactly?
- Who remains without what if the reader does not act?
- Why does this exact offer change that result?
- What question is alive after every major block?
- Did the product enter because the story demanded it?
- Did any invented detail strengthen what the reader believes about the product?
- Did any observation become a recommendation or testimonial stronger than its source?
- Did I invent a competitor weakness or uniqueness claim?
- Does the copy contain only the short hyphen?
- Did I preserve the applicable Michael v5 capabilities without imitating its surface?
- Did I write natively in the campaign delivery language rather than the operator language?
- After the peak, can the reader explain the cost without action, why it repeats and why this offer is worth the supported friction?

- mechanically clean but emotionally flat;
- a character wrapper around a product presentation;

- a proof claim stronger than its source relationship;
- a candidate that regresses below the applicable Michael v5 capabilities;
